How the text follows the surface and becomes part of the mesh

The letters are not projected straight down from the anchor, and they are not added as a separate piece. The surface around the anchor is unfolded flat, the text is placed on that flat map, and the letters are mapped back onto the surface, where they become part of the same mesh.

1. The idea in three steps

Think of peeling a label off a bottle: the label keeps its size and shape on the flat table, and goes back on the bottle without stretching. The same works for a cone, a plate, or any gently curved shape.

3. Making it one mesh

Once the letters are on the surface, they have to become part of it. This is the top view of the unfolded mesh; the same steps apply to any surface because the mesh is already flat on the map.

  1. Unfold a patch of the mesh around the anchor into a flat map, keeping distances along the surface as true as possible.
  2. Place the merged letter outlines on the map: start point and starting direction come from the anchor.
  3. Cut the mesh triangles along the outlines. Each cut is a straight line inside one flat triangle, so the surface itself does not change. The outline becomes ordinary mesh edges.
  4. Classify every piece: inside a letter or outside.
  5. Lift the inside by the height along the surface's own direction at each point. For engraving, push it down instead. Each outline point gets two copies, one that stays and one that moves, and wall triangles join them.
  6. Check that the result is one closed surface, that nothing intersects, and report the worst stretch of the letters.

4. What the anchor does, and what it does not

The anchor decides
  • where the text starts (its centre or corner, set by align)
  • which way the text starts to read (the direction angle)
The anchor does not decide
  • the direction of the mapping
  • the direction of the lift
  • how a letter tilts: that comes from the surface under that letter

6. Limits

  • Distortion on doubly curved shapes. A cylinder, cone or plate unfolds with no distortion. A sphere or a saddle cannot be flattened without some stretching. For small text it is small, and the report gives the worst stretch so the plugin can warn or refuse.
  • Corners. Crossing a single fold is fine, like bending a label. A corner where three faces meet cannot be flattened, so text over it is refused or warned about.
  • Engraving depth. It has to stay shallower than the wall behind it. The plugin checks this by looking inward from each point.
  • Patch quality. The area around the anchor must be an ordinary connected surface without holes or broken triangles; otherwise the plugin says so and does nothing.
  • Coarse meshes. The cuts are exact, but the letter tops copy the mesh's own facets, so a very coarse mesh gives faceted letters, like the object itself.
  • Measured in the M0 spikes (see spikes/RESULTS.md): the unfolding is ARAP started from a flat projection, with a limit of about 10 to 15 thousand triangles in the patch.
